PART 1 — THE PRODUCT, EXPLAINED LIKE A HUMAN
================================================================================

1.1  THE ONE-PARAGRAPH PITCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Signalcraft is a free browser terminal that watches 120+ crypto pairs across
11 major exchanges and tells you two things most people figure out too late:
how the crowd feels about a coin right now, and where a trade would actually
get you in, out, and stopped out. Every signal ships as a finished plan —
entry, stop-loss, take-profit, minimum 1:2 risk-reward — tagged with one of
three risk levels so you always know what you're touching. No account. No
app. No paywall. You open the link and it's just... there.

1.2  WHAT YOU SEE WHEN YOU OPEN IT (WALKTHROUGH)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
First thing that hits you: a live ticker with prices from 11 centralized
exchanges — Binance, Coinbase, Kraken, OKX and the rest — scrolling across
the top like a newsroom.

Below that, the signal board. 120+ pairs, sorted from the strongest setup to
the weakest, 20–30 per page so you're never staring at a wall of noise. Each
card shows the pair, direction (long/short), confluence score, a sentiment
reading from 0 to 100, and a risk badge: green, yellow or red.

Click into a card and it opens up: the sentiment drawer breaks down the news
and social mood — score, 24h social volume, bull/bear split, the headline
that's moving things, and a live wire to the global Fear & Greed index. A
second click draws you a candlestick chart with the entry line, the stop and
the target painted right on the candles. A third click opens the dossier:
the full indicator breakdown in copy-paste format.

Filter by sector (majors, layer-1/2, DeFi bluechips, AI & compute, memes and
on-chain), filter by risk, search any pair, sort by confluence, sentiment,
risk or recency. Switch the whole interface between German, English,
Spanish, Arabic and Chinese with one click. Change the theme if the default
dark isn't your thing.

And the whole thing runs in a browser tab. Phone, laptop, tablet, whatever.

the timeline gets. That bluntness is the product.

5.1  BLOG POST #1 — "You don't need another signal group"
     (~700 words; pair with shots/02_terminal_top_en.png)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title: You Don't Need Another Signal Group. You Need a Better Board.

Every cycle, the same shop opens: a Telegram channel, a countdown sticker,
a screenshot of a 40x with the entry conveniently cropped out. And every
cycle, people learn the same lesson — the product was never the signals,
it was you.

So here's a different shape for the same need. Not a group. A board.

A board is where you go to look, not to obey. Signalcraft was built as
exactly that: 120+ crypto setups laid out best-to-worst, each one carrying
its own reasoning, its own mood reading, and its own risk label.

The mood reading is the part people underestimate. Each pair gets a
sentiment score from 0 to 100, blended from news and social momentum —
X, Telegram, Reddit — and wired into the global Fear & Greed index. Price
tells you what happened. Sentiment tells you what people are feeling while
it happens. Seeing both at once changes the texture of your decisions. You
start noticing when a coin pumps on a 40 sentiment — a move the crowd isn't
convinced about — versus one backed by a 90.

Then the risk label. Three colors. Green for majors and spot names. Yellow

5.2  BLOG POST #2 — "Reading the crowd" (~600 words)
     (pair with shots/04_sentiment_open.png)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title: Reading the Crowd: Why Sentiment Belongs Next to Price

Ask three traders about a coin and you'll get three moods dressed up as
analysis. The uncomfortable truth is that most of us sense the market's
feelings first and build the thesis second.

Fine. Then let's at least measure the feelings.

Signalcraft's sentiment score compresses the noise — posts on X, Telegram
chatter, Reddit threads, news headlines — into one number from 0 to 100
per pair, refreshed against the global Fear & Greed index. The drawer on
each card opens the full picture: the score, the 24-hour social volume,
the bull/bear split, and the headline acting as the catalyst.

Used naively, sentiment is a contrarian toy. Used properly, it's context.

A 90+ reading on a major with price still coiling tells you the crowd is
positioned and impatient — momentum has fuel. A 90+ with price already
vertical tells you the fuel is spent and you'd be buying someone else's
exit. Same number, opposite meaning. The score doesn't decide for you; it
tells you which question to ask.

The same goes for the lows. A coin at 20 sentiment is not automatically a
buy — but a majors name with collapsing social volume and a green risk
badge is exactly where patient people start doing homework instead of
panic-scrolling.

What makes the feature stick is placement. Sentiment lives on the same
card as entry, stop and target. You never get the mood without the plan,
or the plan without the mood. That pairing quietly trains a habit: feel,
then structure, then size.

The crowd is always talking. The edge is in reading it with a number on
the page instead of a knot in your stomach.

— AndrexTheDev (analysis, not advice)

Title: Risk Isn't a Vibe. It's a Traffic Light.

Nobody plans to trade memes like majors. It just happens — a green candle,
a loud timeline, and suddenly the position sizing you reserved for bitcoin
is sitting in a coin that launched on a Thursday.

Signalcraft's answer is deliberately dumb: three colors.

Green means majors and spot-grade names. Yellow means swing trades and
layer-1s with real ecosystems and real volatility. Red means memes,
on-chain gems, and everything else where the chart is mostly a crowd
psychology experiment.

The badges don't predict. They classify. And classification is the part of
risk management that actually survives contact with excitement. When the
label is on the card before you feel anything, "this is a red-badge trade"
becomes a complete sentence — one that carries your position size inside
it.

The structure continues below the badge. Every setup ships with entry,
stop-loss and take-profit at a minimum 1:2 risk-reward, drawn on a
candlestick chart you can open with one click. The stop isn't a suggestion
in paragraph nine of a Telegram message; it's a line on the candles, at the
same visual weight as the target.

None of this removes risk. Red-badge trades can moon and green-badge trades
can bleed. The point is that you chose the bucket before the market chose
your emotions. That's the entire skill, and a traffic light is enough to
practice it.

So when someone asks what the three levels are for, the honest answer is:
they're a mirror. The market was always going to do what it wants. The
badge tells you what you were about to do.

— AndrexTheDev (education, not advice)
